The whole idea

Most messages don't need a big model. So most messages don't get one.

A single large model can do everything, and bills you for everything on every "hi". Swaylo AI classifies each message first and spends only what that message costs.

Somebody typesalmost everything → The small fast model. A price, a delivery question, an address, "is it available". Thousands of short exchanges, and nearly all the traffic stops on this row and never costs more.
A voice note → The model that can hear, and only for as long as it takes to listen. It transcribes what was said, in Hindi, Malayalam, whatever was spoken, then hands the words straight back to the cheap one to answer.
A photo → The model that can see. It says what the picture shows, and if there is an address handwritten on it, it types that address out so the cheap model can use it like any other text.
A provider falls over → The other provider's cheapest model, automatically, in the middle of a sentence. A dearer or dumber answer beats no answer, and somebody who just clicked an ad never hears silence.

How it works

Four decisions, taken on every single message.

Not configured once and left alone. It runs again from the top each time somebody says something.

01

It classifies

Before anything answers, it works out what kind of message this is: words, speech, or a picture.

02

It routes

To the cheapest model that can do that particular job. Vision and speech are paid for only when they are actually used.

03

It speaks as one

Three models can touch one conversation and it still sounds like one assistant, with one memory and one tone.

04

It survives

If a provider stops answering it switches again, to the other one's cheapest model, without dropping the thread.

What the engine gives you

Cheap by construction, not by settling for a worse model.

The saving comes from asking the expensive models less often, not from asking them for less.

It hears

Voice notes are transcribed, in Indian languages, including an address read out loud into the phone.

It sees

Photos are read and described, and handwriting in them is typed out, so a scrawled address becomes usable text.

Ten Indian languages

It answers in the language it was written to, in that language's own script, or in Latin letters if that's how they typed.

Two providers, one voice

It is not tied to one vendor. If one stops answering, the conversation carries on somewhere else.

It degrades, it doesn't stop

The worst outage produces a cheaper answer, not an unanswered customer.

No keyword lists

No menu tree, no regex to tune, no "press 1 for". It judges the message; that is the whole mechanism.
